Changing Lanes, Turning, and Parking

An interactive lesson with short teaching sections, driving scenarios, and a knowledge check. Work through each module, then take the quiz.

1. Changing lanes safely

A lane change should be planned and gradual. Search for a safe gap, communicate your intention, check blind zones, and move only when the space is clear.

  1. Search. Check ahead, to both sides, behind you, and in your mirrors. Identify a safe gap.
  2. Signal. Let other road users know you intend to move left or right.
  3. Recheck the blind zone. Look in the direction of the lane change immediately before moving.
  4. Move smoothly. When there is enough space, steer gently into the new lane and adjust speed to match traffic if necessary.
  5. Finish the maneuver. Cancel the signal once established in the new lane, then recheck your speed and position.
Remember: Mirrors alone do not show every area beside or behind your vehicle. Check the blind zone before moving.

Common lane-change errors

Forgetting the signal
Other drivers may not know what you plan to do.
Moving too soon
Enter only after confirming there is enough space.
Oversteering or slowing unnecessarily
Use a gentle, controlled movement and keep traffic flow in mind.
Leaving the signal on
Cancel it once your vehicle is established in the lane.

Quick scenario

You signal to change lanes, but a car appears in your blind zone during your final check. What should you do?

Reveal the safest response

Stay in your current lane, allow the vehicle to pass, and try again only after checking for a safe gap.

2. Turning with control

Prepare early so you can focus on the turn itself. Signal, search for people and traffic, choose the proper position, and reduce speed before turning.

  1. Plan ahead. Check signs, signals, lane markings, and the road layout.
  2. Signal in advance. The chapter assessment notes that many states require a signal at least 100 feet before a turn in an urban area; follow the law where you drive.
  3. Position your vehicle correctly. Move into the appropriate lane or turning position when safe.
  4. Finish braking before steering into the turn. Enter at a speed that allows you to maintain control.
  5. Check the path. Look for pedestrians, bicyclists, and other road users, and yield when required.
  6. Complete the turn smoothly. Stay in the proper lane and check your position as you exit the turn.
Key idea: Complete your braking before you turn the steering wheel into the turn. Avoid rushing or making abrupt steering movements.

Quick scenario

You reach the corner faster than expected and have not reduced speed enough. Should you brake hard while sharply turning?

Reveal the safest response

Do not rush the turn. Reduce speed in a controlled way before turning whenever possible, and enter only at a speed that allows control. Keep scanning for people and traffic.

3. Reversing direction: turnabouts

Before choosing a way to turn around, consider traffic density and speed, street layout, visibility, and your line of sight. Use a location that gives you enough space and a clear view.

Before any turnabout

  • Check traffic in front, behind, and to both sides.
  • Check mirrors and blind zones, and look for pedestrians, bicyclists, and motorcyclists.
  • Signal your intention and yield to people who have the right-of-way.
  • Move slowly and stop if your intended path is not clear.

Backing into a driveway on the right

  1. Select a suitable driveway or alley on your right.
  2. Check traffic in all directions and check the blind zones.
  3. Signal right; the chapter describes briefly tapping the brake pedal to alert drivers behind you.
  4. Stop about 3 feet from and parallel to the curb, with the rear of your vehicle slightly beyond the driveway.
  5. Shift to Reverse and check traffic again. When clear, look over your right shoulder and back slowly while turning the wheel sharply right.
  6. Continue checking your surroundings and move slowly. Never assume the path is clear just because it was clear a moment ago.
Safety first: If you cannot see enough to complete a turnabout safely, choose another location rather than forcing the maneuver.

4. Parking and securing the vehicle

Parking requires low-speed control, awareness of pedestrians and moving vehicles, attention to signs and signals, and a secure vehicle before you leave.

Entering or leaving a space
Move slowly, keep scanning the whole area, and yield to traffic already in the main lane when leaving a parking space.
Use visual references
Mirrors can help you judge how far forward to pull into a space. Keep checking around the vehicle.
Signs and signals
Follow posted signs, arrows, lights, and parking-area traffic controls.
Watch for movement
Assume drivers and pedestrians may not see you. Be ready to stop if someone backs out or steps into your path.

Parking on a hill

  • Set the parking brake and shift to Park when parking an automatic vehicle.
  • Downhill beside a curb: position the vehicle parallel to the curb, about 6 inches away, then turn the front wheels sharply toward the curb and allow the front tire to rest gently against it.
  • Uphill beside a curb: position the vehicle parallel to the curb, turn the front wheels sharply away from the curb, and allow the vehicle to roll back slowly until the front tire rests against the curb, controlling movement with the brakes.
  • No curb: point the front wheels toward the edge of the road so a rolling vehicle is more likely to move off the road rather than into traffic.
  • Consider curb height and shape, and always secure the vehicle before exiting.
Dutch Reach: Use the hand farthest from the door to open it. This encourages you to turn your body and look for approaching cyclists or traffic before opening the door.

Leaving a downhill curbside space

  1. Back up 2–3 feet.
  2. Turn the front wheels away from the curb.
  3. Search for traffic approaching from behind and signal.
  4. When clear, steer into the nearest travel lane, then check behind and adjust speed.
